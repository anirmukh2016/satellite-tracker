How It Works ▲

SGP4 (Simplified General Perturbations 4) predicts satellite positions from TLEs — compact orbital parameter sets updated twice daily by NORAD. It treats the orbit as an ellipse, then corrects for Earth's bulge (J2) and atmospheric drag. It runs in your browser and recomputes the ISS every 2 seconds. Full walkthrough in the blog →


The ISS: the model is exaggerated (the real station is ~109 m) and kept a constant size on screen. It faces the way it flies: nose along its direction of travel, solar-array truss across the orbit plane. Hover or tap it for live details.


Day / Night: the night side is shaded using the real position of the Sun, with a soft twilight band at the terminator. The HUD says whether the ISS itself is sunlit or in Earth's shadow (the model dims).


In-View Zone (off by default): the green ring is where the ISS is at least 10° above the horizon. It is filled in only where you could actually see it right now: sky dark (Sun below −6°) and ISS sunlit. No fill means nobody inside the ring can see it. It ignores weather and brightness.


Trails float at the ISS's altitude:
■ Past 30 min   ■ Future 30 min
They are drawn in the Earth-fixed frame, so they turn with the planet. A small arrow on each trail, some distance from the ISS, points the way the ISS is travelling: the orange past path leads up to the ISS and the cyan future path leads away from it.


Ground Track & Nadir (off by default): the dashed orange/cyan line is the orbit projected onto the surface — the last orbit and the next ~2.5, so each lap shows up shifted ~23° west. The thin white dashed line is the nadir line, straight down to the point beneath the ISS.


Time bar: drag the slider to move up to three hours either way and watch the terminator, the zone and the ISS move. Live returns to now; Share copies a link that restores your toggles, location and (if you scrubbed) the time.


Next Passes: set a location (your device, a click on the globe, or typed coordinates) to list the ISS passes you could see with your own eyes in the next 30 days: when it rises, its peak height and where in the sky it travels. Tick “Show all passes” to include the ones that are in daylight or in Earth's shadow.


Keyboard: P past trail · F future trail · D day/night · Z zone · G ground track · E ECI · C ECEF · R recenter · L live · ←/→ ±1 min (Shift ±10).


ECI frame: fixed to distant stars. SGP4 outputs position here. Its axes (solid) never rotate.


ECEF frame: rotates with Earth. Its axes (dashed) and a dashed prime-meridian arc are carried round with the planet. GMST (θ) is the rotation angle between ECI-X and ECEF-X, increasing by 360° every 86,164 seconds. With both frames on, a yellow arc shows θ. The axes continue (fainter) through the globe to the origin, the Earth's centre, where they all meet.


Both frames use the same colour per axis (X Y Z). The white arrow is the ISS position vector — the same vector in both frames, just with different components (see the data panels). The faint ring is the equatorial plane.


Data: Celestrak (wheretheiss.at fallback) · Orbit math: satellite.js (SGP4) in your browser
Frontend: Three.js r160 (vendored) · Updates: every 2s
